# Competitor scan (October 2026)

Desk research to position the Orthanc Analytics website. Method: web search results, page snippets and listings. Direct page fetches were blocked in the research environment, so **navigation, cookie banners and language options were not verified live**: check them in a browser before quoting this document externally. Figures are the competitors' own claims or third-party reports, not verified by us.

## Palantir: the design and structure reference

- **Navigation:** Platforms (AIP, Foundry, Gotham, Apollo), Impact Studies, Offerings, AIPCon, Newsroom, Careers, with one persistent "Get Started" call to action.
- **Look:** near-black hero with very large type, a monochrome palette, thin dividing lines and lots of whitespace.
- **Proof:** customer outcomes (Impact Studies, keynote videos) rather than feature lists. Each product is a named system with a one-line definition. Copy is short and assertive.
- **Trust:** a dedicated Privacy & Civil Liberties section with an engineering team, an advisory council and published whitepapers. Ethics is presented as engineering, not small print.
- **Investors:** a separate site (investors.palantir.com).
- **Caveat:** Palantir is controversial in Europe (e.g. the German Constitutional Court's 2023 ruling on Hesse's police data analysis). We borrow the restraint, not the associations, and never mention Palantir on our site.

## What the category does

- **Campaign software:** navigation by customer type, demo + public pricing or trial, "X organisations in Y countries" counters, GDPR shown as "EU hosting", nonpartisan stance.
- **US partisan data firms:** proud alignment, scale of voter files, contact-form calls to action.
- **Italian pollsters:** media visibility as proof, contact page only.
- **Almost nobody** publishes methodology, data sources, accuracy back-tests or a trust centre.

## Where Orthanc can stand out (applied on the site)

1. **"Places, not people."** Territorial intelligence with no voter files, which fits EU rules on political advertising. *Used in the hero, Method and FAQ.*
2. **Provenance as a product feature:** sources, checksums, human approval, honest empty states. *Method, Trust and pipeline sections.*
3. **An openly right-of-centre European vendor.** European tools are mostly nonpartisan or progressive. *Positioning line.*
4. **Municipal history since 1946 as a product,** where Italy mostly offers articles and consulting. *Evidence strip and Polis page.*
5. **Dated, sourced claims.** Every number has a caption; the "Why now" facts are dated and linked.

## Recommendations not yet implemented (decide later)

- **Published accuracy back-tests** (e.g. 2022 general and 2024 European elections), as election.de does. Only with real back-test code and results.
- **"Download a sample report"** as a second call to action (a real, anonymised PDF from Orthanc Polis).
- **Packaged offers** (e.g. a "campaign sprint" for a region), with prices on request.
- **Use-case pages:** party HQ, regional campaign, candidate, think tank.
- **A Trust Centre page** that grows the Trust section: data-flow diagram, sample audit log, AI policy, data-processing agreement template.

## Regulatory notes for copy (not legal advice)

- **Regulation (EU) 2024/900** (transparency and targeting of political advertising) has mostly applied since 10 October 2025. Article 18 restricts targeting and ad-delivery techniques that use personal data. Don't market targeting; never claim to be "exempt".
- **AI Act:** AI systems intended to influence election outcomes or voting behaviour are high-risk (Annex III). Tools for organising, optimising or structuring campaigns from an administrative or logistical point of view are excluded. Describe Orthanc Polis as planning and resource allocation.
- **Italian law on polls** (L. 28/2000, art. 8) bans publishing polls in the 15 days before a vote. Never call our projections "sondaggi", and be careful with any public projection near election day.
- **Unverified superlatives** ("#1", "most accurate") have been banned by courts for competitors (e.g. Civey in Germany).
